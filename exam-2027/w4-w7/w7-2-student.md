## 25–45 分｜文章帶讀（先限時 6 分鐘）

> When a city library noticed fewer teenagers visiting, it asked students what they wanted from a reading space. Some preferred printed books because writing notes on paper helped them focus. Others liked digital books because they could read them on a phone. The library decided to provide both formats. It also opened a small discussion area where teenagers could recommend books to one another. Three months later, more teenagers were visiting. However, the library staff could not say which change made the biggest difference. The director suggested continuing to ask students for feedback and trying different solutions. In her view, listening to readers was more effective than assuming that one format suited everyone.
